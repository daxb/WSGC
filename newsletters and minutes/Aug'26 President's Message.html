President's Message August 2026

Dunkel Takes the Title in a 37-Hole Final

Greetings to our West Seattle membership and fellow golfers this fine summer of 2026. It's been a few months since we updated you on the season, what lies ahead, and some fun golf happenings at the course above Fauntleroy Creek and below Camp Long.

First off, the Club Championship concluded this week: a knockdown, dragout 37-hole donnybrook between three-time champion Andrew Coghill and longtime member Eric Dunkel. Here is the result, in Andrew's words:

It was another glorious day at West Seattle Golf Course on August 18th for a 36-hole club championship match to remember.

We were fortunate to play both rounds with semi-finalist Andy Wood and 2025 Club Champion Milo Stover. Colin Gants checked in on us several times throughout the rounds to offer encouragement, and our club president Ted Irwin hopped on Dunkel's bag for the second 18. Their extra interest and support added another memorable element to an already wonderful day on the course.

Eric set the tone early in the first round, birdieing 4 and 6 for an outward nine of 35. He added birdies on 14 and 15 for a 69 and a three-up lead heading into the final 18. In the heat of the afternoon round, he did what he needed to do to get the win, ending his second straight match on the first sudden-death playoff hole.

My hat's off to Eric for passing the test of this summer-long six-round event to earn the title of Club Champion.

— Andrew Coghill, runner-up

For the runner-up to have the grace and class to write about a match he lost tells you everything you need to know about Andrew Coghill. And the deserving champion, Eric Dunkel, was due for the title — his ball-striking talent and unique approach to the game have impressed us all in previous tournaments, like when he shot a 71 with only three clubs (and no putter) a few years back. A fabulous final indeed.

Also of note, veteran Larry Daniels eked in a short putt to beat Patrick Distefano by one shot in the 36-hole Senior Men's Club Championship. This event is getting more and more competitive as the demographics age.

A New Tournament This November: The Coyote Howl

One of my initiatives that I brought to our board was to add one more stern test of golf, will, and determination to our schedule. This is not a direct replacement for the pro shop's Devil's Nassau, which has been laying in a coffin for several years, but it will still be a very difficult day of golf with a long layout in the belly of mid-November weather.

Expect the tees somewhere near the back of the blacks (close to 7,000 yards), and the hole locations tucked on aggravatingly sloped edges of our greens. No wacky crisscross holes to navigate — just straightforward, painful golf that will make many of us howl, much like the local coyote population.

This tourney will have a limited field due to place of play, and a shotgun start. No senior or ladies tees. We recommend higher handicaps avoid the carnage.

On the docket, we still have Fall Field Day in September and the 666 in October as well. The treasurer reports the club remains in a solid financial position, and there will also be a Holiday Party.

Course Drainage Work — and Plenty of World Cup Sand

You probably noticed course workers busily carving up various trouble spots that tend to get soggy (and stay soggy). Our fearless superintendent, Rocky, has kept records and is focused on fixes by removing the turf, adding sand layers, and resodding or seeding as needed — most notably on #9, #10, #18, #5 and others.

Since sand isn't cheap, this was a nice windfall from the city; Lumen Field removed the material after the World Cup. Our club is also supporting the course maintenance crew with a pizza lunch for their hard work.
